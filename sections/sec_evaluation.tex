\section{Empirical and Comparative Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

With \ourapproach now defined (Section~\ref{sec:causal_impact}), this section gives
the essence of what the rest of the paper establishes: how \ourapproach compares
against the dominant feature-attribution baselines, against actual causality and
Pearl's probability of causation, against a causal-effect attribution method, and how
it behaves as the model grows from textbook size to a deployed system. Each result is
given at a glance, one claim, one figure, the headline number, with a pointer to
where it is developed in full: the formal comparisons in
Sections~\ref{sec:shap_examples}--\ref{sub:pearl_actual_cause_prob}, and the complete
protocols in the appendices. A reader who wants the shape of the argument can stop
here; a reader who wants the mechanics can follow either pointer.

\subsection{\ourapproach matches actual-causality verdicts on canonical archetypes}
\label{sec:eval_verdicts}
On the discrete structural models where actual-causality verdicts are available, \ourapproach should
reproduce its verdicts; on the canonical overdetermination and undercutting
patterns it should separate a genuine cause from a preempted one. We test this on a
synthetic structural model carrying three archetypes at once, linear
necessary-and-sufficient, overdetermined, and preempted (gated), together with an
irrelevance control, with an analytic ground truth throughout
(Appendix~\ref{sub:synthetic}). Across two contrasting factual cases, all ten
archetype desiderata hold: \ourapproach scores the active cause above the
preempted one, keeps the irrelevant control at the noise floor, and, unlike the
binary actual-cause verdict, does so with graded magnitudes that extend to
continuous variables. The same agreement holds on Pearl's desert-traveller, where
\ourapproach reproduces his within-scenario ranking
(Section~\ref{sub:pearl_actual_cause_prob}, Appendix~\ref{app:desert}).

\subsection{SHAP and Causal SHAP}
\label{sec:eval_shap}
Section~\ref{sec:shap_examples} works through this in full on two worked examples
(OBCB and the signal-with-mediation chain), with the formal SHAP and Causal SHAP
definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:signal}; here is the verdict it reaches.
Both plain and Causal SHAP \emph{assign attribution backward}, against the arrows of
the causal graph (they do not satisfy the zero desiderata D-YX/D-YM/D-MX, with Causal
SHAP's interventional correction recovering only D-YX), and both \emph{tie} the
direct and indirect causes of an outcome (D-MXY), because they decompose a
prediction's departure from a population mean rather than the realized outcome.
\ourapproach{}'s realized-outcome reference and witness mechanism break both
symmetries, separating the direct cause from the indirect one at every instance.

\subsection{The Differential Causal Effect}
\label{sec:eval_dce}
The Differential Causal Effect (DCE) attributes an outcome change to features via
local derivatives of the structural map. On a model with a non-monotone internal
response (Appendix~\ref{sec:DCE}), DCE and \ourapproach can disagree on sign and
magnitude where the local gradient is unrepresentative of the counterfactual
contrast \ourapproach integrates over. \ourapproach tracks the realized-outcome
contrast rather than a point derivative, which is what keeps its attribution
aligned with the structural role of each feature. The full contrast is shown over a credit-limit grid in Appendix~\ref{sec:DCE} (Figure~\ref{fig:contrast}).


\subsection{Scaling past exact actual causality}
\label{sec:eval_scaling}
Actual-cause computation requires enumerating counterfactual subsets, which is
exponential; \ourapproach replaces enumeration with a Monte Carlo thin search.
On a controlled family of problems (Appendix~\ref{sec:ac_benchmark}), exact subset
enumeration times out at about $17$ variables, whereas the necessity-only
\ourapproach estimator continues to roughly $73$--$145$ variables under an
undemanding sample budget and a per-size compute cap of $60$ minutes, sustaining a
correct-attribution rate above $0.85$ up to about $60$ variables while visiting
several orders of magnitude fewer cause--witness configurations.

\subsection{Continuous, dynamical outcomes}
\label{sec:eval_sir}
Actual causality offers no verdict when the outcome is continuous and the causes
interact through a dynamical system. On a Bayesian SIR model with two
non-pharmaceutical policies and a threshold (peak-overshoot) outcome
(Appendix~\ref{sec:sir_benchmark}), but-for analysis cannot separate the two
policies, whereas \ourapproach assigns lockdown roughly twice the responsibility
of masking, with the gap driven by the necessity term under witness pinning.

\subsection{A real-world deployed model}
\label{sec:eval_avm}
Finally, \ourapproach runs on a deployed automated valuation model (AVM) with
machine-learned components trained on millions of points (a regime where actual
causality is intractable and SHAP is the practical default)
(Appendix~\ref{sec:avm}). \ourapproach returns a structured, context-sensitive
attribution across the model's internal variables, whereas SHAP concentrates
almost all responsibility on a few downstream variables and places little weight
on the rest.

Sections~\ref{sec:shap_examples}--\ref{sub:pearl_actual_cause_prob} develop the comparative claims above in full before \cref{sec:discussion} draws out the implications.
