### CURRENTLY IN PROGRESS — Insertion 4 — drafted, NOT YET APPLIED (STALE — SEE ABOVE)
Placement: after Cardinality-Constrained Uniform Selection `\end{example}`, before

Approved text (verbatim):

> \medskip\noindent\textit{Informally, the variable selection distribution $\Gamma$
> encodes prior beliefs about which subsets of suspects are worth examining. A uniform
> distribution treats all suspect subsets as equally plausible; a cardinality-constrained
> choice focuses the search on interactions of a particular order, analogous to choosing
> which interaction terms to include in a regression.}
>
> For the OBCB analysis we use a uniform $\Gamma_s$ over the three non-empty subsets of
> $\mathbf{S}$: $\{\varname{gender}\}$, $\{\varname{credit}\}$, and
> $\{\varname{gender}, \varname{credit}\}$, each with probability $1/3$. With only two
> suspects there is no prior reason to privilege any combination, so the uniform choice is
> the unique symmetric default. $\Gamma_w$ is uniform over
> $\{\emptyset, \{\varname{check\text{-}failed}\}\}$, each with probability $1/2$.
> In larger suspect sets the choice of cardinality range becomes substantive: sampling
> uniformly from the full powerset yields an expected intervention size of
> $|\mathbf{S}|/2$, which spreads attribution across large coalitions and may make
> individual feature contributions harder to isolate; we return to this design decision in
> the experimental section.
